# Ottoman sandbox art evidence

Original native geometry for **Süleyman's reign, 1520–1566 CE**, with an Istanbul/Edirne commercial and domestic vocabulary. The civic role is a **han courtyard and entry**, not a mosque. Ordinary buildings use modest timber/plaster rooms, stone footings, shops, working courts and hipped tile roofs. There are **no minarets or architectural domes** in this kit, no Baroque palace, no nineteenth-century fez, and no copied religious inscription or imperial emblem.

Read with [[Civilization Building and Army Reference]], [[Miniature Civilization Redesign]] and the approved redesign change. Sources checked October 29, 2026. All geometry, colors and simple vessel markings are original. Historical appearance is a candidate for review, not a measured reconstruction of one settlement.

## Dated source cards and gaps

| Card | Primary source / period | Interpretation and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| O1 · Commercial han | Turkish Ministry of Culture and Tourism, [Rüstem Paşa Caravanserai, Edirne](https://edirne.ktb.gov.tr/TR-76567/rustempasa-kervansarayi.html/); Sinan survey project, [building entry](https://sinanasaygi.org/en/eserler.asp?ID=165&action=eserDetay), attributed **c. 1560–1561?** | Two-storey rooms, courtyard-facing galleries, ashlar and brick support selective civic masonry and entry arches. The ministry identifies Süleyman-era patronage; the survey preserves uncertainty about the exact construction year. The current hotel includes a **1972 restoration**, so modern hotel fabric is not copied. Our small han entry, room/wing arrangement and exchange counter are invented. |
| O2 · Contemporary city/domestic imagery | Matrakçı Nasuh, *Beyân-ı Menâzil-i Sefer-i Irâkeyn-i Sultân Süleymân Hân*, **1537**, Istanbul University T.5964, fols. 8b–9a; reproduced/identified in Gülru Necipoğlu's original [Harvard-hosted study](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/gnecipoglu/files/necipoglu_g._the_aesthetics_of_empire._arts_politics_and_commerce_in_the_construction_of_sultan_suleymans_magnificence.pdf), fig. 4a | Period city views establish a mixed settlement of ordinary roofed buildings and monuments. They are stylized court imagery, **not** measured domestic plans or proof of our precise timber joints, shutters or hipped-roof pitch. No securely identified ordinary 1520–1566 house elevation has been reconstructed here. Later Safranbolu timber houses and Baroque Bosphorus houses are not silently used as period models. |
| O3 · Observer chronology | National Gallery of Denmark, [Melchior Lorck chronology](https://www.smk.dk/article/biografi-lorck/) and [museum research publication](https://www.smk.dk/en/article/melchior-lorck/) | Lorck made observations/sketches in Constantinople **1555–1559**, while many resulting prints were made/published later. The museum stresses interpretation and fiction as well as observation. This bounds the general period/visual reference; it does not certify our garment, domestic plan or every later print as a direct 1550s record. |
| O4 · Ceramic workshop output | The Met, [Iznik Dish with Floral Design, 56.185.2](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/453599), **c. 1545–1560**, and [dish 14.40.727](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/446648), **mid-sixteenth century** | Secure period evidence for ceramic production and a light/blue palette. Our plain blue-center dishes are new abstractions, not copies of motifs. Iznik stonepaste and glazing are technically more complex than the game's generic **clay → pottery** recipe. The workroom, circular brick firing chamber, bench, fuel, extraction site and kiln elevation are invented; these objects do not prove a particular workshop building. |
| O5 · Period civic/care distinction | UNESCO, [Historic Areas of Istanbul](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/356/), dates the Süleymaniye complex **1550–1557**; Turkish Ministry, [Istanbul monuments](https://www.ktb.gov.tr/EN-99272/istanbul.html), identifies its hospital and other functions | Demonstrates that care was one function in a larger period institution. The game's small treatment room and shaded couch are invented, **not** a replica of Süleymaniye's hospital. Its sacred monumental dome/minarets are not borrowed for warehouses, homes or a commercial han. Later Selimiye (1569–1575) is outside the selected reign and is excluded. |
| O6 · Blade | The Met, [saber 36.25.1297](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/24320), **1522–1566**, probably Istanbul | A curved blade and guard support the melee silhouette. It is an opulent surviving example with a **later replacement grip**, not proof of a standard soldier's weapon. Our undecorated grip/blade is invented. No gold, gemstones or inscriptions are copied. The early court yatagan is not represented as the later widespread eighteenth/nineteenth-century janissary sidearm. |
| O7 · Bow and equipment context | The Met Arms and Armor Department, [Islamic Arms and Armor](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/islamic-arms-and-armor), contextualizes Ottoman sixteenth-century equipment and recurve bows | Supports a bow candidate alongside blade/polearm equipment, but is broader than one dated Ottoman bow. Our limbs, string, quiver and foot-archer pose are abstractions. No claim that Ottoman forces lacked firearms, or that every soldier used this uniform/weapon. No firearm or mounted gameplay is added. |

Primary evidence is strongest for dated monumental/commercial context, surviving ceramics and blade forms. **Ordinary domestic construction, exact workshop architecture, farm/storage arrangements and the support uniform remain explicit gaps.** The plain timber/plaster/stone choices are restrained interpretations. Roofs are genuinely hipped native facets, rather than a relabeled Spartan gable; this is not a claim that every Ottoman building had one roof type.

## Complete role manifest

Models use the shared authoritative footprints, are authored full size and scaled once to **0.25**. Every BasePart and roof WedgePart is anchored, noncolliding, nontouching and nonqueryable. The kit contains no Humanoid, Tool, script, combat registration, production logic or saved state.

The three **passive** roster candidates have plain sleeved coats, cloth sashes and wrapped cloth headwear: curved-saber `melee`, recurve-bow/quiver `ranged`, and unarmed cloth-satchel/linen `medic`. These simplified garments do not reconstruct a named military regiment, royal kaftan or universal healer uniform. BodyColors, actual body colors and the established warrior face match the existing Ottoman NPC art defaults; the palette does not describe every person in the empire. Existing imported tending-worker clothes remain provisional if used by the current renderer.
