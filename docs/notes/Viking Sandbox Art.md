# Viking sandbox art evidence

Original procedural native art for **Denmark and southern Scandinavia, 900–1050 CE**. This is a regional game vocabulary, not a reconstruction of all Scandinavian buildings or an assertion that “Viking” named one homogeneous people. Curved timber longwalls, sloping roof supports, segmented thatched roofs, modest daub work sheds and enclosed working yards distinguish this kit from Spartan tiles, Egyptian flat roofs and Persian courts. No horned helmets, stone castles, stave-church towers or fantasy dragon-roof houses are introduced.

Read with [[Civilization Building and Army Reference]], [[Miniature Civilization Redesign]] and the approved `miniature-civilization-redesign` change. Sources were checked October 29, 2026. No museum photograph, inscription, texture or source model is copied. The muted wood, wool, straw and earth palette and all proportions are original art choices.

## Region, period and source cards

| Card | Museum / archaeology evidence | Interpretation and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| V1 · Homes | National Museum of Denmark, [Viking homes](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/the-people/viking-homes/) describes timber or clay-lined walls, roof-bearing posts, sloping outside supports, pitched thatch/wood roofs and roof smoke openings | Longhall and work-shed forms stay within this Danish vocabulary. Roof pitches, dimensions, curvature and separate functional buildings are invented. The dark smoke-opening cue is a surface abstraction, not a working ventilation system; no later masonry chimney appears. |
| V2 · Curved halls | National Museum of Denmark, [Trelleborg fortresses](https://natmus.dk/historisk-viden/danmark/oldtid-indtil-aar-1050/vikingetiden-800-1050/magt-og-aristokrati/trelleborg/), late tenth century; Trelleborg Museum, [reconstructed longhouse](https://trelleborgvikingeborg.dk/oplevelser/langhuset) | Curved longwalls, long halls and sloping supports inform the civic and household silhouette. The museum explicitly corrects the upright supports in the **1942 reconstruction** using later excavation evidence. Our supports slope; the kit does not copy the reconstruction's details or claim its thatch is the documented original roof covering at Trelleborg. |
| V3 · Farm and storage | National Museum of Denmark, [Viking food](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/food/); Viking Ship Museum, [Food in Viking times](https://www.vikingeskibsmuseet.dk/frontend/Dokumenter/Food_2_EN.pdf) | Agriculture, cereal food, seasonal preservation, grain querns and containers support an ordinary grain plot and storage role. Crop spacing, fenced plot, raised store floor and storage chests are readability choices, **not** an excavated Danish granary plan or a later Norwegian stabbur reconstruction. Food sources themselves caution about later written descriptions. |
| V4 · Wood and craft | National Museum of Denmark, [imports and local production](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/expeditions-and-raids/imports/?force=desktop) and [axes](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/weapons/axes/); Wikinger Museum Haithabu, [museum teaching handbook](https://haithabu.de/file/gudrun_lehrkraefte_final_lowres.pdf) | Woodworking, local smiths, workshop waste and practical tools distinguish craft/supply activity from a palace. Log stacks, bench, chopping block, shed arrangement and firing bed are invented. The workshop in this shared economy produces **pottery from clay**, not iron; evidence for smiths is not used to pretend that clay turns into metal. |
| V5 · Ceramics | Swedish History Museum, [ceramics across the trading network](https://vikingar.historiska.se/objects.php?e=&l=en&showcase=8d6e22fb-8d27-4e9c-9899-8fd211e989f7) and [domestically manufactured Birka clay pot, object 3249924](https://vikingar.historiska.se/object_details.php?e=no&l=en&object=2e6bd9df-38d9-44e1-854d-e4d9dd72a3ac) | Locally made and imported ceramic vessels both existed. The Birka vessel is an eastern Scandinavian comparison; its particular grave is not given a precise date by this record. Plain rounded hand-formed pots, clay gathering and a low firing arrangement are interpretations, not a documented Hedeby kiln. No fast industrial wheel or Greek domed kiln is asserted. |
| V6 · Exchange | National Museum of Denmark, [trade in the Viking period](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/expeditions-and-raids/trade-in-the-viking-period/?force=desktop) | Hedeby and Ribe provide southern Scandinavian market/craft context. A trading board and cloth booth in front of the council hall combine game functions. The generic coin balance is an abstraction of payment, not a claim that every transaction used uniform modern coins. |
| V7 · Clothes | National Museum of Denmark, [clothes and jewellery](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/the-people/clothes-and-jewellery/) | Wool/flax, tunics, trousers, belts and leather shoes inform the figures. Muted tunic colors, sleeves, simple boots and hair are original variations; no universal fur armor or elite silk uniform. Preserved clothing is fragmentary and costume varies by status, age and region. |
| V8 · Infantry and archer | National Museum of Denmark, [axes](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/weapons/axes/); Swedish History Museum, [warrior equipment and Birka Bj 581](https://vikingar.historiska.se/objects.php?e=&l=sv&showcase=e7729e8d-e49b-48cd-9418-ec1a6dffa25c) | Axe, round wooden shield, iron boss and bow are period equipment candidates. Our short one-handed axe is **not** a reconstructed long two-handed Danish axe. The bow/quiver and practice targets are simplified props. The Swedish grave is a regional comparison, not proof of a universal Danish roster or standard military drill yard. No automatic combat or axe throwing is implemented here. |
| V9 · Helmet | University of Oslo, Museum of Cultural History, [Gjermundbu and Yarm helmets](https://www.khm.uio.no/om/aktuelt/aktuelle-saker/vikinghjelmen-fra-yarm.html) | A plain iron cap and narrow nose guard provide a restrained helmet cue. It does not reconstruct either specimen or imply all warriors owned helmets; the examples are Norwegian/North English comparisons. No horns, wings or elaborate imported Greek armor. The museum's updated account also avoids repeating the obsolete claim that only one Viking-period helmet is known. |
| V10 · Care | National Museum of Denmark, [Viking health](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/the-people/health/) | Evidence for injury and illness motivates a support role but does **not** establish a purpose-built army hospital or an exact plant-treatment tradition. The healer hut, outdoor couch, linen package, satchel and specialist uniform are gameplay inventions. No modern medical cross, magical effect or healing behavior is added. |

The source region is deliberately Danish/southern Scandinavian for construction. Swedish pottery/equipment and Norwegian/North English helmets are explicitly labeled comparisons rather than silently combined into a single excavated settlement. Icelandic turf farms, later medieval storehouse ornament, stave churches and imported Bronze Age horned helmets are excluded.

## Role manifest

All roles retain the shared authoritative footprints. Models are authored full size and scaled once to **0.25**. Every BasePart is anchored, noncolliding, nontouching and nonqueryable. No source rigs, Humanoids, Tools, scripts, actor registration, production authority or saved state are contained in the kit.

| Kind · footprint in studs | Distinct native geometry | Evidence / remaining invention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `civic` · 10 × 8 | Broad curved-wall timber hall, sloping supports, variable-width pitched thatch, roof smoke cue, entrance boards and modest cloth exchange booth | V1/V2/V6. Combining council and trade functions is a game choice. No fantasy royal castle. |
| `house` · 6 × 6 | Shorter household longhall, curved timber wall/post rhythm, thatched roof, chest and clay pot | V1/V2/V5. The compact catalog footprint compresses an elongated house; housing four workers is a balance rule. |

| `timber` · 8 × 6 | Stacked felled logs, sheltered carving bench and chopping block | V4. Exact supply-yard plan is invented. |
| `material` · 8 × 6 | Open clay gathering bed, low earth banks, basket and hand-formed pot | V5. Extraction layout and shared material budget are gameplay abstractions. |
| `workshop` · 8 × 6 | Small thatched timber/daub shed, working board, hand-formed pottery and low stone-lined firing bed | V4/V5. Firing layout is interpretive. The unchanged paid recipe is clay → pottery; no false iron-production recipe. |
| `storehouse` · 8 × 6 | Modestly raised plank floor on low stone footings, thatched open-front shed and grain chests | V3/V4. Not a reconstruction of a later decorated Norwegian storehouse. |
| `training` · 10 × 8 | Earth practice court, low fence, three plain wooden shield targets and small rest shade | V8. Targets, shelter and permanent drill function are readable game inventions. |
| `healer` · 6 × 6 | Small timber/thatched hut, outdoor plank treatment bench, linen cover, folded cloth and pot | V1/V7/V10. Dedicated military treatment building is unverified and supplies no healing behavior. |

Three passive roster silhouettes use the existing Viking NPC art palette and warrior face: axe/round-shield `melee`, self-bow/quiver `ranged`, and unarmed satchel/linen `medic`. BodyColors and actual body-part colors agree. The art default is not a statement that everyone in Viking-Age Scandinavia had one skin tone, hair color or uniform. Imported tending-worker clothing remains provisional if rendered by the existing worker service.
