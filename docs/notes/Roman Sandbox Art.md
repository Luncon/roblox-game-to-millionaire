## Sources and phase exclusions

**Housing:** the official [Ostia archaeological park's House of Diana panel](https://ostiaantica.cultura.gov.it/pannelli-didattici-e-mappe/larea-centrale-e-i-complessi-di-rappresentanza/casa-di-diana/)
dates the courtyard apartment complex to Hadrian's period. It describes street
shops, several possible storeys and a porticoed central court. The model
compresses those features to a small, partly two-level residence. It does not
reproduce the full apartment block, claim that every worker owned a domus, or
copy the later second-century fountain, late-century paintings, third-century
raised floors or late Mithraeum. Brick texture and simplified roof layout are
artistic choices; exact ancient roof heights are not established by this panel.

**Civic, storage and military buildings:** English Heritage's
[Housesteads history](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/housesteads-roman-fort-hadrians-wall/history/)
identifies the original Hadrianic granaries and says the surviving main plan
largely belongs to the end of Hadrian's reign. It also records major later
rebuilding, including granary work in 205–208. Its
[site description](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/housesteads-roman-fort-hadrians-wall/history/description/)
distinguishes headquarters, granaries, barracks and a **probable** courtyard
hospital. Our role allocations borrow this vocabulary. They exclude the page's
post-270 chalet-barrack illustration, late-third-century Building 15 warehouse,
later residential conversions, fourth-century towers and medieval bastle.
Ventilation, buttress arrangement, upper walls and tile roofs are interpretive
renderings of functional types, not claims to reconstruct their precise first
Hadrianic elevation. The arcaded civic frontage is an original design choice.

**Food:** the Vindolanda Trust's [grain evidence and tablet discussion](https://www.vindolanda.com/blog/food-and-drink-part-1)
describes cereal remains and food deliveries; tablet 649 is dated 93–102 CE.
This supports a grain-supply role near the start of our roster period. Some
environmental finds belong to later fort phases and do not date our field plan.
We deliberately do not use Housesteads' surrounding field systems as a 125–160
plan: English Heritage dates them probably after 200. Crop strips, threshing
pad, height and season are gameplay abstractions, not a documented field layout.

**Material and pottery:** the City of London's
[Highgate Wood archaeology summary](https://www.cityoflondon.gov.uk/things-to-do/green-spaces/highgate-wood/roman-kilns-in-highgate-wood)
places production between about 50 and 160 CE, including second-century pottery.
The conservation project's [kiln description](https://www.highgateromankiln.org.uk/kiln.php)
distinguishes later updraught kilns with an upper flue from earlier ditch kilns.
Our kiln/fire-channel arrangement is a readable original abstraction. It is not
the modern replica and does not copy its restored fabric. Greyware forms fit
the site's described household production; the clay-settling trough and drying
slab need more specific period/site evidence before historical acceptance.

**Timber and tools:** the dated [Corbridge Hoard, 122–138 CE](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/corbridge-roman-town-hadrians-wall/things-to-do/the-corbridge-hoard/)
contains armour, weapons, tools and writing equipment. English Heritage's
[teachers' collection guide, p. 53](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/siteassets/home/learn/teaching-resources/resources-library/hadrians_wall_teachers-kit-2022.pdf)
identifies carpentry/masonry and medical tools in the site's displays. These
support distinct supply/craft/support roles, but do not document our exact
trestle, frame saw, workshop layout or medical uniform. A specific dated saw
and early-imperial medical case comparison remain review gaps.

## Building manifest and interpretation cards

All nine roles retain the shared economic budget. No price, output, capacity,
research gate or recipe changes are embedded in the art module. Native geometry
is authored once at full dimensions and scaled to 0.25. Footprints below include
roofs, arches and props; all parts are anchored and nonblocking. The Roman kit
uses brick massing, segmented arcades, a stepped store and cellular ranges,
instead of recolouring the Spartan one-room plaster/portico family.

| Kind / world footprint | Original Roman geometry | Source-supported comparison / interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `house` · 6 × 6 | Tall rear residential range, lower roofed side rooms, central court/drain and brick entrance arch | Hadrianic Diana's court and multilevel residence; compressed plan, drain, upper windows, roofs and four-worker housing allocation are invented. |
| `civic` · 10 × 8 | Paved headquarters court, rear administrative range, three freestanding segmented arches and stone exchange counter | Housesteads headquarters function; arcade, simplified roofing and combined merchant/research role are interpretive, not a temple facade. |
| `farm` · 8 × 8 | Three long cultivated strips, spelt-like stalks, circular threshing surface and wooden measure | Vindolanda cereals and supply documents; exact crop appearance, permanent standing grain and layout are invented. |
| `timber` · 8 × 6 | Sawn-plank stacks, trestle bench and upright frame saw | Corbridge tool/carpentry context; frame, wood species and site arrangement are provisional designs. No claim of mechanized sawing. |
| `material` · 8 × 6 | Brick-lined settling trough, wet clay, working/drying slab and vessels | Highgate ceramic production supports the clay role; exact preparation installation and geometry remain unsourced interpretations. |
| `workshop` · 8 × 6 | Tall round updraught kiln, top flue/fire channel, open potter's shelter, wheel and greyware | Later Highgate kiln type and wheel-thrown ware; dimensions and arrangement are original abstractions rather than a measured reconstruction. |
| `storehouse` · 8 × 6 | Raised horreum on supports, deep side buttresses, low ventilation slots, loading steps and long tile roof | Original Hadrianic storage function; elevation, vents and buttress spacing are provisional rather than copied from the 205–208 rebuilding. |
| `training` · 10 × 8 | Long cellular barrack range and an open sand drill court with four plain posts | Hadrianic barrack function; tiny range, court and exercise props are invented. No post-270 chalet plan or late Vegetian target dimensions are copied. |
| `healer` · 6 × 6 | Masonry rear treatment range, side benches/couches, basin and arched entrance | Housesteads' probable hospital comparison; reduced plan, linen and furnishings are interpretation, retaining uncertainty about the source's function. |

The restrained red tile, brick, stone and plaster palette is art direction;
surviving buildings do not establish one empire-wide paint scheme. The role
shapes and economic abstraction should not be read as historical workforce,
social status or labour systems. Existing supplied tending-worker clothing is
provisional if reused and has not been certified as Roman dress.

## Presentation actors

The three original figures use geometry cloth, anchored parts and the existing
Roman NPC skin/warrior-face art defaults. BodyColors match actual skin parts;
the palette is fictional character art, not a claim about all Roman people.
There are no Humanoids, Tools, scripts, damage/healing statistics or combat
registration. Nothing is imported from an original NPC/equipment template.

- **Frontline candidate:** short gladius, curved rectangular scutum, banded iron
  cuirass and brim/neck-guard helmet. The
  [Corbridge armour record](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/corbridge-roman-town-hadrians-wall/history-and-stories/collection)
  dates segmentata remains to 122–138. The Royal Ontario Museum's
  [early-imperial sword discussion](https://www.rom.on.ca/blogs/re-enactment-archaeology-and-ancient-rome-greece-weekend-ii-iv-sword)
  identifies the first–second-century short gladius. Helmet, shield curvature,
  red tunic, fittings and resting grip are simplified designs needing a more
  exact 125–160 equipment comparison; the later Dura shield's decoration is not
  copied. This candidate is legionary-inspired while the Housesteads comparison
  is an auxiliary fort; it is not a portrait of its actual garrison or a claim
  that all auxiliaries wore segmentata.
- **Missile candidate:** short recurved bow, string, back quiver, longer tunic
  and stepped conical helmet. The
  [Roman Army Museum's Hamian archer discussion](https://romanarmymuseum.com/about/the-syrian-archers-on-hadrians-wall/)
  cites evidence in Britain from 122 and discusses bow/quiver equipment. Its
  Housesteads relief is a comparison whose exact carving date is not resolved
  here. Costume colour, simplified helmet and resting grip remain original art.
  Syrian service in the Roman army is not represented as a uniform ethnicity
  for the empire. No mounted capability or firing is implemented.
- **Support candidate:** pale tunic, wooden medical case and rolled linen. The
  Corbridge collection guide supports medical tools, not our outfit or specific
  field organization. A permanently selected healer is a game role; no modern
  cross insignia, historical uniform claim or healing ability is introduced.

## Verification boundary

`RomanKit.available(kind)`, `create(kind)` and `createActor(role)` match the
Spartan factory contract: return an unparented model at floor-origin, scale 0.25,
with civilization/role/evidence attributes and no interaction behavior.
`World` remains responsible for selection proxies, ownership, actor counts and
placement. This delivery does not change mappings, state or production services.

`tests/miniature-game/RomeScene.server.luau` checks every native building and actor,
four cardinal rotations, footprint/floor bounds, inert flags, body/equipment
alignment and source fingerprints. It compares normalized geometry against
Sparta while ignoring names, colours and materials, so a recolour cannot pass.
It reports `MiniatureRomeSceneResult` and destroys every temporary model. Its
result explicitly distinguishes geometry checks from integrated gameplay and
visual review; it never selects a civilization or writes player state.
