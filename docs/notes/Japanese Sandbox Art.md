# Japanese sandbox art evidence

October 29, 2026. **Native contracts, the integrated UI tour and gallery review
pass; historical interpretation remains provisional.** Japan retains the game's 1550–1600 Sengoku/Momoyama identity.
Ordinary domestic/craft references centre on Ichijodani in Echizen before its

## Sources, observations and exclusions

**Ordinary houses and roofs:** excavation/reconstruction researcher Yoshioka
Yasuhide's [Ichijodani reconstruction account, pp. 5–7](https://www.nara.accu.or.jp/el/textpdf/Structure_Reconstruction_in_Historical_Site_Development_(2006).pdf)
explains evidence for narrow town plots, gable/ridge posts, split-panel shingles,
half-timber/mud/plaster walls and sliding doors. Stones/logs/planks retaining
shingles are supported by comparative paintings. The reconstruction combines
finds and inference; its modern restored houses are not surviving sixteenth-
century superstructures. Our compact floor plan, posts, slopes, stones and
proportions are original abstractions. We chose shingles for these town buildings,
following this evidence, rather than applying generic thatch or castle tiles to
every ordinary roof. Workshop/store/treatment roofs share that modest family;
this does not establish every role's exact excavated roof.

**Town crafts and medicine:** the official
[Ichijodani museum site walk, stops 14–17](https://asakura-museum.pref.fukui.lg.jp/site/walk)
describes mixed craft/store plots, bentwood-working evidence and a probable
physician residence identified through preparation tools and medical-book
fragments. These support function-specific props, not our exact tiny shop or
clinic plan. The museum identifies its present Karamon at stop 22 as an Edo
construction; that gate is excluded. No reconstructed shop is treated as an
unaltered ancient original, and no later Edo-Tokyo house or store is imported.

**Selective civic keep:** Matsumoto's official
[keep-structure account](https://www.matsumoto-castle.jp/about/tower)
gives the city's 1593–1594 construction view for the Great Keep, Northwest Tower
and Roofed Passage. Its [chronology](https://www.matsumoto-castle.jp/about/history/chronological)
distinguishes the circa-1634 Moon-Viewing/Southeast additions. We retain only a
small original black/white keep-and-stone-base vocabulary, reducing the number
of storeys and adding a detached plain trade counter. No later moon-viewing
wing, red viewing balcony or Edo-period castle-town layout is copied. The model
is not the actual Matsumoto plan, an exact date claim about each surviving
surface, or a universal dwelling for townspeople. Ordinary buildings remain
low timber structures rather than palaces or pagodas.

**Pottery and clay:** [Echizen town's official ware history](https://www.town.echizen.fukui.jp/event/02/p006369.html)
describes large medieval single-chamber tunnel kilns, household vessels and
increased Sengoku demand. It differentiates the early-Edo change to paddy-field
clay and surface treatment; those later details are excluded. Our source clay
is a generic hillside/preparation role. The small mound/tunnel representation
compresses a kiln landscape, not a surveyed kiln. It avoids the later standard
multi-chamber climbing-kiln staircase. Tub, bank, fire-mouth and workbench
arrangements are interpretations; a kiln on every town lot is not implied.

## Nine role cards

Native models are authored at full dimensions then scaled to 0.25. Footprints
include roof eaves, stones, posts and work props. All geometry is anchored and
nonblocking; interaction/ownership targets belong to World. Economic budgets,
research gates, recipe quantities and capacities stay shared with other kits.

The two civic roof levels are a limited landmark treatment. Domestic shingle
roofs, ridge posts, timber lattice and modest masses distinguish this family
from a Ming tiled civic-courtyard kit; full cross-kit silhouette review is still
required when both are integrated. Grey shingle/tile, timber and plaster colours
are art direction, not a uniform national paint scheme. Any reused supplied
farm-worker clothing remains provisional and has not been certified as Japanese.

## Period troop candidates

- **Frontline:** plain yari shaft with a simplified three-point head, lacquer-
  coloured cuirass/lacing and restrained helmet/neck lames. Birmingham Museum
  of Art's [Shimosaka three-pointed spear, about 1600](https://www.artsbma.org/collection/three-pointed-spear-jumonji-yari/)
  provides a dated weapon lead at the end of our window. The
  [Tokyo National Museum armour list](https://www.tnm.jp/modules/r_exhibition/index.php?controller=item&id=6916&lang=en)
  contains broad sixteenth–seventeenth-century examples, so our plain helmet
  is only a simplified period candidate, not a certified 1550 uniform. Personal
  crests, later spectacular helmets and automatic katana-as-primary assumptions
  are avoided; no shield purchase or shield is introduced for Japan.
- **Missile:** an asymmetric tall yumi, string, arrows and back case. Hikone
  Castle Museum's [wood-and-bamboo bow](https://hikone-castle-museum.jp/en/collection/2025.html)
  is dated to the late sixteenth century, with the related inscription evidence
  pointing after 1588. Length and resting grip are simplified for a miniature
  silhouette. The model does not claim a functioning bow, cavalry or an exact
  copy of Ii Naomasa's personal equipment.
- **Support:** pale simple clothing, medicine case and preparation bowl. The
  physician-site comparison supports a preparation role; the exact garment,
  portable kit and permanently recruited support capacity are game invention,
  with no medical efficacy or historical uniform claim.

All three are original geometry-clothed passive figures, with no Humanoid,
Tool, scripts, combat registration, attack/healing statistics or imported source
rig. Skin parts/BodyColors and the warrior face match existing Japanese NPC art
defaults; those are fictional character palettes, not population-wide claims.

## Validation and historical limits

`JapaneseKit.available(kind)`, `create(kind)` and `createActor(role)` return the
same floor-origin, unparented, inert 0.25-scale contract as the other kits, with
`CivilizationId=japan` and the evidence-card path. World supplies interaction
proxies, ownership, worker/roster counts and cardinal placement. This delivery
does not edit mappings, profiles, shared balances or the production build.

`tests/miniature-game/JapanScene.server.luau` waits for the injected fake-store
boundary and checks all nine building/three actor roles, footprint/floor, native
scale, four rotations, inert flags, equipment/body pose preservation, source
fingerprints and geometry distinct from Sparta/Rome. It uses per-part measured
tolerances, reports `MiniatureJapanSceneResult` and destroys temporary art without
selecting a civilization or writing state.
