# Mauryan sandbox art evidence

October 29, 2026. **Native contracts, the integrated UI tour and gallery review
pass; historical evidence gaps remain open.** The game identity is Mauryan India, 320–250 BCE.
The reference centre is the middle Gangetic region, especially Pataliputra, with
explicitly qualified comparisons elsewhere in early historic northern India.
This is not a reconstructed palace, village, religious complex or uniform army.

Read with the [historical matrix](Civilization%20Building%20and%20Army%20Reference.md)
and [building-kit contract](../../openspec/changes/miniature-civilization-redesign/specs/civilization-building-kits/spec.md).

## What the sources establish

**Civic structure and chronological exclusions.** The official
[Bihar Kumhrar site account](https://tourism.bihar.gov.in/in/en/home/destinations/patna/kumrahar-puratav-park)
identifies the 80-pillared hall and wooden platform. It inconsistently presents
the hall as royal and Buddhist in different passages; neither precise function
is asserted by our model. A nine-pillar miniature court borrows the combination
of plain stone supports and timber floor/roof construction at a radically
compressed scale. Pillar count, roof cover, span, pitch, height and merchant
furniture are invented. The source does not establish a surviving domestic roof.

Crucially, the same account dates the monastery/hospital Arogya Vihar to the
**fourth–fifth centuries CE**. It is excluded from this kit. No Gupta monastery,
Mughal dome/minaret, later Sanchi gateway, post-Mauryan temple tower or modern
visitor shelter is used as a 320–250 BCE ordinary house/store/clinic.

**Domestic/workshop evidence is limited.** The original ASI
[1911–12 Bhita report, pp. 29 onward](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/13565.pdf)
and its [available opening-page scan/transcription](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Annual_Report_of_the_Archaeological_Survey_of_India_1911-12.pdf/72)
describe a site with many later phases. Only the opening page and indexed report
excerpts were retrievable during this pass; the complete domestic plates were
not inspected and must not be treated as reviewed evidence. They do **not**
justify importing a complete Gupta/Kushan house into our earlier period.
Modern institutional discussion of Bhita's domestic phasing also varies.
Rectangular earthen compartments, veranda posts and the precise small-house
plan here therefore remain conservative art interpretations, not claims that
an exact Mauryan plan or roof has been established at Bhita.

**Roof decision:** timber framing and a modest plant-fibre covering provide a
readable provisional material family. The gently bowed, faceted roof is an
original design and is marked `RoofInterpretation=true` on roofed models. Its
curve, lashings and thatch texture are **not archaeologically verified Mauryan
domestic details**. ASI's [Sudama cave description](https://asipatnacircle.gov.in/index.php/monuments-details)
dates the rock-cut cave to Ashoka's twelfth year and describes a vaulted inner
chamber; it does not prove that an ordinary detached house had that same roof.
The kit does not convert sacred rock-cut geometry into every economic building.
Further measured domestic/workshop evidence is required before task 7.5 can be
declared historically accepted.

**Ceramics:** the British Museum's
[Northern Black Polished Ware record](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/term/x72914)
identifies wheel-thrown fine ware, dark lustrous surfaces and production in the
middle Ganges plain across a broader 600–200 BCE range, especially 400–300.
It supports a period-overlapping ceramic vocabulary, not a uniquely Mauryan
invention. Black-slipped display bowls are one class among ordinary wares;
the game does not imply every household vessel was elite polished ware. The
mixing bed, low firing clamp and wheel arrangement are invented pending a
specific excavated production installation. No Roman updraught kiln is reused.

**Food, garments and troop comparisons:** the primary ancient account
[Arrian, Indica](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/ancient/arrian-bookVIII-India.asp)
describes agriculture, wrapped garments, long bows, hide shields and healers.
Arrian wrote in the second century CE, drawing partly on Nearchus and other
accounts associated with Alexander's late-fourth-century-BCE campaign. These
are comparative reports with temporal, regional and outsider limitations,
not a specification of a Pataliputra regiment or clinic in 320–250 BCE.
Claims about extraordinary weapon power and medical success are not translated
into game stats or modern health guidance. Exact crop/field, dress, equipment
and healer organization remain interpretations.

## Nine building cards

All footprints include eaves and props at quarter scale. All parts are anchored,
noncolliding, nonqueryable native geometry, with no scripts or interactions.
Ownership, select targets and workforce counts remain the World renderer's job.
Economic prices, goods, recipe ratios, capacity and research gates are unchanged.

| Kind / world footprint | Original geometry | Supported lead / interpretation boundary |

| `house` · 6 × 6 | Unequal earthen compartments under separate light roofs, exposed veranda posts and a woven floor mat | Modest earthen/timber domestic family is provisional; Bhita's complete domestic plans and roof details are not claimed as securely dated/reviewed. |

The earthen palette, plain cylindrical civic supports, light bowed covers,
unequal house masses, storage bins and low firing bed form a separate geometry
family from the Spartan plaster/gable and Roman brick/arcade kits. Palette
choices are art direction rather than an empire-wide historical paint scheme.
The supplied tending-worker clothing, if used by World, remains provisional.

## Three passive role candidates

- **Frontline:** plain spear and tall narrow hide-colored shield, wrapped cloth
  and head cloth. Arrian's described spears/shields are a comparative lead;
  exact blade, shield shape, proportions and this combination remain invented.
- **Missile:** simple tall bow, string, long arrows and bundle case. This differs
  from the Roman recurved bow. Long-bow height has the qualified Arrian lead;
  grip, construction, case and garment folds are original simplified art.
- **Support:** plain wrapped cloth, preparation pouch and bowl. This is a
  presentation role; the equipment does not assert a specific historical
  medical institution, modern healing efficacy or a uniform healer costume.

All three figures use colored geometry cloth without a Humanoid, Tool, scripts,
combat registration, attack or healing stats. They retain the existing Mauryan
NPC skin/face art defaults, with consistent BodyColors/actual body parts. These
are fictional palette defaults, not claims about every historical person.
Factories create original geometry and never mutate an imported source model.

## API and verification boundary

`MauryanKit.available(kind)`, `create(kind)` and `createActor(role)` match the
other kits: an unparented floor-origin model, scale 0.25, civilization/role and
evidence attributes, with selection/ownership supplied separately by World.

`tests/miniature-game/MauryaScene.server.luau` waits for the ready injected-fake
boundary, checks nine building/three actor roles, native scale/footprint/floor,
four rotations, inert flags, body/equipment alignment and source fingerprints.
It compares geometry with Sparta and Rome while ignoring colors/names, and
uses measured per-part tolerances for native floating-point transforms. It
reports `MiniatureMauryaSceneResult`, destroys all temporary art, and never
selects a civilization or changes player state. Its native result is recorded
in the integration milestone log linked below.
