# Ming sandbox art evidence

October 29, 2026. Original procedural models for **Ming China, 1400–1600 CE**,
in the separate miniature fake-store sandbox. The built environment is a
**regional Huizhou/Jiangnan interpretation**, not a uniform architectural style
for all Ming territories. These are readable game designs, not measured copies
of nine excavated buildings. Read with [[Civilization Building and Army Reference]],
[[Miniature Civilization Redesign]] and [[Miniature Economy Implementation]].

## Sources and period checks

- **Dated domestic and hall evidence:** the primary
  [Qiankou conservation monograph excerpt, Science Press](https://www.ecsponline.com/book/2017/yz/9787030553768-001001-curved-sam.pdf)
  identifies Sijian Di as a 1495 hall and Fang Guantian's house as an ordinary
  mid-Ming residence with brick/timber construction, small grey tiles and stepped
  gable walls. [Hefei University of Technology's architectural survey](https://ctjzwh.hfut.edu.cn/2023/1029/c14323a297305/page.htm)
  describes the latter's restrained construction and records later alterations
  in other houses. The collection was relocated and conserved in the modern
  period. Its assembled museum arrangement is not an intact Ming village plan.
  Our small skywell house and modest civic hall interpret these dated forms;
  neither copies an ancestor hall's religious function or a whole family compound.
- **Regional context and a dating limit:** [UNESCO's Xidi/Hongcun account](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1002/)
  describes a long-lived Anhui village tradition spanning the fourteenth through
  twentieth centuries. It supports the regional context, but its present-day
  photographs cannot certify that every carved detail or roof is Ming. Later
  Qing residences, monumental gates and rebuilt ensembles are not treated as
  direct evidence for this kit's selected period.
- **Structural comparison:** the Philadelphia Museum of Art's
  [Zhihuasi ceiling record, about 1444](https://www.philamuseum.org/objects/44151)
  provides a dated Ming timber-architecture comparison and identifies possible
  Kangxi-period additions. The game uses a few plain stacked bracket blocks in
  its civic portico. It does not reproduce the religious ceiling, its dragon
  imagery, later additions or the museum's reconstructed surround. Ordinary
  houses and work sheds do not receive monumental bracket arrays.
- **Craft production:** Quan Kuishan's primary
  [Jingdezhen imperial-kiln excavation report abstract, Palace Museum Journal 2013](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2018/06/05/u5b1662a99ebb9.pdf)
  reports Ming kiln groups including gourd-shaped furnaces, walls and associated
  production remains. The workshop uses a small rounded chamber, firebox, short
  flue and drying bench. This is an original miniature abstraction, not a
  reconstructed imperial workshop. The mixed Ming/Qing site's chronology does
  not authorize copying later kilns. Plain pale vessels with a small blue rim
  do not claim an excavated vessel profile, reign mark or imperial porcelain
  quality. The shared one-clay-to-one-pottery recipe is fictional.
- **Bow and clothing candidates:** the Palace Museum's primary
  [Ming court handscroll record](https://minghuaji.dpm.org.cn/paint/detail?id=9hv8wtoav5a5hyr0l7pmjc765dwesqti)
  includes archery among its scenes. Its traditional identification with the
  Xuande emperor is debated, with a Chenghua identification also discussed;
  both fit the selected broad period. It supports a Ming court comparison, not
  a universal infantry uniform. The National Palace Museum's
  [Departure Herald and Return Clearing record](https://www.npm.gov.tw/Exhibition-Content.aspx?l=2&sno=04007243)
  offers a further primary Ming procession reference. Our spear guard and archer
  remain simplified candidates; exact helmet, plate pattern, bow dimensions and
  rank-specific clothing require closer equipment review.

The Palace Museum's [current Arms Gallery page](https://www.dpm.org.cn/pavilion/247424.html)
principally presents Qing objects. Its later imperial bows, helmets and horse
equipment are not used as Ming certification. Royal yellow roof tiles, Qing
queue hairstyles and Japanese castle ornaments are not universal substitutions
for the selected regional architecture or clothing.

## Nine-role manifest

The shared catalog's footprint, price, worker slots, goods and research effects
remain authoritative. Grey tiles, white plaster, enclosing stepped walls and a
skywell court create the domestic identity. The production roles vary their
massing and props rather than repeating the civic roof on every building.

| Kind / footprint | Visible design | Evidence and interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `civic` · 10 × 8 | Grey tiled hall and lower portico, white stepped boundary walls, restrained red timber posts, small bracket blocks, paved court and trade counter | Dated Huizhou hall/material vocabulary with selective timber-architecture comparison. Combined council/merchant function, plan and colors are invented. |
| `house` · 6 × 6 | Rear room and two low wings around an open skywell, dark tile courses, high stepped side walls and a court drain | Mid-Ming domestic comparison. Tiny dimensions, one-storey silhouette, drainage and four-worker housing capacity are game abstractions. |
| `farm` · 8 × 8 | Low banks, shallow water-colored field panel and rice-like clumps | A regional southern agriculture interpretation. Exact planting, crop stage and irrigation layout are not reconstructed; no functional water system or new good is added. |
| `timber` · 8 × 6 | Open grey tiled shed, squared beam stock, joinery bench and unfinished frame | Timber construction is evidenced; yard organization, tools and lumber species remain interpretive. |
| `material` · 8 × 6 | Partitioned clay settling bed and prepared clay blocks | Original clay supply area, not an excavated processing layout. It does not imply that porcelain production needed only one material. |
| `workshop` · 8 × 6 | Low tiled workroom, rounded firing chamber, small firebox/flue, raised drying bench and pale vessels | Primary kiln comparison with deliberately simplified original geometry. Exact scale and firing technology remain unverified. |
| `storehouse` · 8 × 6 | Taller enclosed grey-roof store on a brick plinth, high ventilation slits, barred door and bins | Functional storage interpretation using the domestic material vocabulary. Not a certified Ming state granary plan. |
| `training` · 10 × 8 | Open earth court, small equipment room, three targets and spear rest | Period archery comparison; dedicated drill site, target design and rack are invented. It does not confer combat abilities. |
| `healer` · 6 × 6 | Modest tiled room, paved care courtyard, treatment bench, plain linen and vessel | Explicit gameplay support interpretation. Building institution, medical uniform and props await specific period evidence. No medicine recipe or healing is simulated. |

The stepped masonry enclosure, skywell and compact court distinguish this kit
from Japan's castle/keep imagery. It contains no stacked keep tower, torii gate or
shachihoko. Shared East Asian timber and tile traditions do not make every roof
interchangeable; these structural choices are regional, selective interpretations.

## Passive figures

`melee` has a plain rounded iron-colored helmet, abbreviated chest plates and a
resting spear. `ranged` has a plain cap, bow/string and quiver. `medic` has a plain
cap, satchel and folded cloth without a weapon. All use a restrained robe, belt,
trousers and shoes. Garment cuts, role colors and equipment proportions are
original art decisions, not verified rank or regiment uniforms. The support role
is a game candidate rather than a reconstructed permanent military specialist.

These original figures have consistent BodyColors/body-part colors and the
existing project warrior face. Their palette is an art default, not a claim about
every Ming-era person. They contain no Humanoid, Tool, scripts, health/damage
stats or combat/healing registrations. No firearm or powder ability is added;
the selection of spear and bow candidates does not deny historical Ming firearms.
They remain independent of workers, whose supplied clothing still needs its own
historical review.

## Verification boundary

`MingKit.create(kind)`, `available(kind)` and `createActor(role)` match the other
kit interfaces. Models are authored full size, scaled once to **0.25**, and carry
this evidence-card path. All art parts are anchored, nonblocking, nontouching and
nonqueryable. Source templates are not imported or edited.

`tests/miniature-game/MingScene.server.luau` creates temporary models without
touching player state. It checks all nine catalog roles and three figures,
footprint corners at four cardinal rotations, native scale, a bounded part count,
grey roofs, selective civic bracketing, skywell/firewall geometry, distinct
production silhouettes, inert actors, body colors and equipment. It destroys its
temporary models and reports `MingScenePassed/MingSceneResult`.

Pinned StyLua and Selene pass for the kit and fixture.
