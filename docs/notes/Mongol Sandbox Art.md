# Mongol sandbox art evidence

Original native geometry for the **Mongol Empire, 1206–1260 CE**, combining household/court/supply felt dwellings with **settled imperial production** informed by Avraga and Karakorum. It does not present the empire as nine identical tents, nor suggest that every mobile household cultivated a permanent grain field or owned a kiln.

Read with [[Civilization Building and Army Reference]], [[Miniature Civilization Redesign]] and the approved `miniature-civilization-redesign` change. Sources checked October 29, 2026. All shapes and muted colors are original abstractions; no museum patterns, photographs, inscriptions or models are imported.

## Period evidence and modern craft distinguished

| Card | Primary source, period and context | Applied interpretation / limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M1 · Period felt dwellings | William of Rubruck's **1253–1255** account, [University of Washington's annotated public-domain translation](https://depts.washington.edu/silkroad/texts/rubruck.html), sections on dwellings, clothing and furnishings | Circular stick frames, white/dark felt, roof opening, ropes and covered storage support the plain shell/coffer vocabulary. A thirteenth-century European missionary's observations have translation, geographic and religious biases. Our roof geometry, proportions, band positions and plain doorway are invented; no exact ornament is reproduced. |
| M2 · Living craft | UNESCO, [Traditional craftsmanship of the Mongol Ger](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-craftsmanship-of-the-mongol-ger-and-its-associated-customs-00872), **inscribed 2013**, with modern community documentation | Shows an ongoing craft tradition involving wood, felt, canvas, ropes, assembly and repair. It is **not** independent proof that every modern crown, lattice joint, painted motif or textile existed unchanged in 1206. The kit's undecorated facets are checked against M1, rather than copying a modern decorated ger or stove. |
| M3 · Settled imperial context | Mongolian Academy of Sciences / University of Bonn / DAI, [Karakorum excavation project](https://www.iak.uni-bonn.de/de/institut/abteilungen/vor-und-fruehgeschichtliche-archaeologie/projekte-1/karakorum) | Construction in 1235 and first-half-thirteenth-century craft activity support work courts beyond gers. Diverse artisans and traders were present, including coerced labor. Low earth wall and timber/felt craft awning are invented superstructures; no Buddhist monument or Chinese palace is assigned to an ordinary workshop. The later **1586 Erdene Zuu monastery** is excluded. |
| M4 · Period crops | Noriyuki Shiraishi, “Searching for Genghis: Excavation of the Ruins at Avraga,” in [*Genghis Khan and the Mongol Empire*, Smithsonian-hosted book](https://repository.si.edu/bitstreams/3b527b70-7323-470d-904f-bf1f03c9f8b5/download), 2009, pp. 132–135, especially fig. 17.8 | Excavated wheat/barley/millet remains, including plant sections beyond cleaned grain, were radiocarbon-dated to the first half of the thirteenth century and interpreted as local cultivation. The crop bed represents an imperial settlement's provisioned economy. Its millet-like heads, channel, spacing and permanent ripe crop remain invented; Avraga evidence is not a measured Karakorum field plan. |
| M5 · Resource and food uncertainties | University of Bonn's current [Urban Impacts research program](https://www.for5438.uni-bonn.de/about/key-areas) | Building ceramics, wood resources and food provisioning are active research subjects. The project reports kilns for building ceramics around Karakorum but says the extent/organization of local farming remains unclear. Our clay extraction and small pottery kiln are role interpretations; **building ceramic kilns are not direct proof of our domestic-vessel recipe or kiln elevation**. No 1307 garrison agriculture is relabeled as 1206–1260 evidence. |
| M6 · City chronology and diversity | Jan Bemmann and Susanne Reichert's original [Karakorum archaeology study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41826-020-00039-x), 2021 | Grounds the distinction between mobile court, imperial settlement and later urban history. It does not turn the open court into an excavated palace or treat every object from the site's two centuries as belonging to the selected fifty-year window. The low supply ger and separate craft court are readable game designs. |
| M7 · Arms candidates | John of Pian de Carpine's **1245–1247** journey, [University of Washington translation](https://depts.washington.edu/silkroad/texts/carpini.html), account of guards at the imperial assembly | Swords, bows and arrows support dismounted blade/bow candidates without importing later firearms. Exact blade curve, bow limbs, quiver, cap and clothing cut are interpretive. There are no horses, mounts, movement skills, archery damage or mounted-combat claims. |
| M8 · Textile status | Cleveland Museum of Art, [Cloth of Gold: Displayed Falcons, 1996.297](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1996.297), **mid-1200s**, Central Asia | Demonstrates a period elite ceremonial textile context. It is **not** a template for a universal soldier's uniform. Our figures use plain cloth coats; no falcon, imperial gold pattern, later Qing robe or modern national emblem is copied. |

The farm/material/workshop silhouettes intentionally differ from the earlier exploratory matrix's purely pastoral/felt-working candidates because this playable slice retains the shared **grain, timber, clay and pottery budgets**. Settled imperial archaeology supplies their context. It does not erase pastoralism, imports or the diversity of work performed by conquered and mobile communities. Civilization-specific goods and recipes remain a future economy decision; this art adds no new recipe.

## Complete role manifest

Authoring is full size, followed by exactly one **0.25** scale operation. All catalog footprints stay authoritative. Every part is anchored, noncolliding, nontouching and nonqueryable; there are no Humanoids, Tools, scripts, mount seats or gameplay stats.

| Kind · footprint in studs | Distinct native geometry | Evidence / remaining interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `civic` · 10 × 8 | Larger, taller council ger, faceted felt roof, exposed low crown and rope bands, separate trade awning/board and coffer | M1–M3. Council/exchange arrangement and size hierarchy are invented; no palace or religious ornament. |
| `house` · 6 × 6 | Compact family ger with plain doorway, circular mat, segmented felt and one covered household coffer | M1/M2. Exact lattice joinery is omitted and proportions are invented. |
| `farm` · 8 × 8 | Open imperial-settlement cereal bed, millet-like heads, narrow water channel and harvest sack | M4/M5. Represents settled cultivation, not a generic mobile camp's universal economy. |
| `timber` · 8 × 6 | Open awning, frame-working bench, bundled roof poles and stored lattice rods | M1–M3/M5. Wood preparation and supply role are interpreted; no automatic local forest abundance. |
| `material` · 8 × 6 | Open clay gathering area, low earth banks, prepared lumps and plain vessel | M3/M5. Specific extraction plan and yield are game rules. |
| `workshop` · 8 × 6 | Non-ger settled earth-wall craft bay, timber/felt awning, potter's working board, exposed firing chamber and vessels | M3/M5. Archaeological context supports craft and ceramics; roof, kiln and domestic pottery layout remain invented. Shared clay → pottery recipe is unchanged. |
| `storehouse` · 8 × 6 | Lower supply ger, three external covered coffers and rolled felt stocks | M1/M2/M6. Separate supply function and balanced capacity are abstractions; no cart or caravan simulation. |
| `training` · 10 × 8 | Open earth practice court, woven targets, weapon rack and modest rest shade | M7. Targets and permanent training institution are original readability props. |
| `healer` · 6 × 6 | Small treatment ger, low felt-covered couch, folded cloth and jar | Plain shelter vocabulary from M1/M2; dedicated healer institution, gear and treatment layout are gameplay inventions, with no healing behavior. |

The three passive figures are **dismounted**: `melee` has a slightly curved blade and plain wrapped coat, `ranged` has a recurved bow/string/quiver, and `medic` has a cloth satchel and folded linen without a weapon. Caps, sashes and coats are unornamented candidates, not reconstructed named garments or claims of a universal uniform. The existing Mongol NPC art skin default and warrior face are used consistently in BodyColors and actual body parts; this does not describe every person of the empire. Existing tending-worker dress remains provisional where that renderer is used.
