# Spanish sandbox art evidence

October 29, 2026. Original native models for the Spanish identity, **1500–1600
CE**, in the separate fake-store miniature sandbox. The architecture combines
selected **Iberian domestic, market and workshop vocabulary**. It is not a
reconstruction of one town, every Spanish region or overseas colonial missions.

## Evidence cards and chronology

- **Domestic patio:** the [Museo Casa Natal de Cervantes patio record](https://museocasanataldecervantes.org/patio-del-museo-casa-natal-de-cervantes/)
  explicitly says the patio supports were timber before its 1956 restoration.
  The current stone columns cannot simply be copied as the original house.
  Our ordinary house has timber posts, a wooden upper gallery, plaster/brick
  rooms and an open patio. Its small proportions, railing and room arrangement
  are invented. The museum's [botica account](https://museocasanataldecervantes.org/botica/)
  also identifies sixteenth-century wall painting and adobe evidence in the house.
- **A dated house, with later interpretation separated:** the
  [Casa Museo Lope de Vega building record](https://www.casamuseolopedevega.org/es/la-casa-museo/el-edificio)
  dates construction to 1578 while explaining later transformations and recreated
  rooms. Lope's own residence there began in 1610, beyond this kit's period.
  The building provides a late-sixteenth-century domestic comparison; its
  reconstructed seventeenth-century furnishings and room allocations are not
  treated as direct evidence for our model.
- **Market and civic vocabulary:** the [municipality of Tarazona](https://www.tarazona.es/tu-ciudad/descubre-tarazona/ayuntamiento)
  records the 1557 decision to build a market, viewing gallery and granary, and
  explains its later use and architectural alterations. Our combined civic/trade
  role uses that functional comparison, not the present facade as a complete
  1550s reconstruction. The [Castilla y León heritage account of Valladolid's plaza](https://www.turismocastillayleon.com/es/patrimonio-cultura/plazas-mayores/plaza-mayor-valladolid)
  dates rebuilding after the 1561 fire and identifies arcades while noting that
  completion and attic changes extend later. The game's low stone arcade,
  brick upper market gallery and tiled halls are original abbreviated geometry.
  No modern town-hall facade, later attic or elaborate imperial frieze is copied.
- **Ceramic work:** the primary architectural/archaeological report,
  [Hernández Valencia and López Martín, Centro Cerámica Triana, revista PH 85, 2014](https://www.iaph.es/revistaph/index.php/revistaph/article/view/3440/3432),
  pp. 109 and 117, distinguishes early kiln remains used through the sixteenth
  century from complete twentieth-century kilns above them. A sixteenth-century
  combustion chamber was incorporated into the visitor route. The kit's small
  round chamber, firebox, chimney and drying bench are interpretive; they are
  not a measured copy of that early chamber or the later factory. The
  [Ministry of Culture's Ruiz de Luna museum account](https://www.cultura.gob.es/cultura/areas/museos/mc/arquitectura-museos/museos-gestion-transferida/castillalamancha/ruiz-de-luna.html)
  supports sixteenth-century Talavera ceramics, while dating the current museum
  building to 1620. That later convent does not supply our workshop architecture.
- **Crossbow:** the [Met's Spanish crossbow record, about 1540–60 and later](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/34904)
  supplies a dated Iberian comparison with explicit later components. The
  [Albuquerque Museum's Spanish ballesta, about 1550](https://albuquerque.emuseum.com/objects/30975/arbalest-or-ballesta-spanish-crossbow)
  gives another directly dated example. Our stock, short metal limbs, string and
  bolt case are original abstractions, without copied inlay, maker marks or a
  functioning loading/shooting mechanism. A crossbow candidate does not imply
  that all late-sixteenth-century Spanish infantry still used crossbows.
- **Frontline silhouette:** the [Fitzwilliam Museum's morion comparison, about 1580](https://data.fitzmuseum.cam.ac.uk/id/object/18322)
  provides a late-period helmet-type comparison. Its catalog identifies North
  Italian manufacture and an almond crown with a backward stalk; it does not
  document our raised longitudinal comb or Iberian manufacture. The kit comb is
  an original interpretation within the broader period helmet vocabulary. The
  catalog distinguishes its
  provenance tradition from established facts; the kit does not turn that
  tradition into proof of an individual Armada soldier. The pike and plain
  cuirass are a broad candidate, not a reconstructed complete panoply. The
  [Defence Library's 1596 Espeio y disciplina militar record](https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/es/consulta/registro.do?id=1119)
  is a contemporary military-literature reference for further equipment review,
  not a claim that our dimensions were measured from that work.

## Role manifest

All nine catalog footprints, prices, worker slots and research effects stay
unchanged. Earthenware uses the same explicit clay/pottery gameplay budget.
Stone arches are selective civic geometry; ordinary domestic and work roles use
timber, brick, plaster and restrained clay-tile roofs.

| Kind / footprint | Visible design | Interpretation and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `civic` · 10 × 8 | Rear tiled hall, three-bay stone arcade, brick upper market gallery, small windows, forecourt and trade counter | Sixteenth-century market/civic comparisons. Combined function, arcade proportions and whole plan invented; no claim to reproduce Tarazona or Valladolid. |
| `house` · 6 × 6 | Plaster/brick room, timber patio posts, upper wooden gallery and railing, low side walls and vessel | Domestic patio comparison. Tiny gallery, exact room arrangement and four-worker housing capacity are gameplay abstractions. |
| `farm` · 8 × 8 | Dry cereal furrows, grain stems/heads and an earthen vessel | Grain is consistent with the market/granary context; crop spacing, species and farm layout are interpretive, with no specific excavated field claimed. |
| `timber` · 8 × 6 | Partial lean-to tile cover, squared timber stock, carpenter's bench and plain hand-plane block | A functional carpentry interpretation. Exact tool, species and yard organization need closer period craft evidence. |
| `material` · 8 × 6 | Clay treading bed, settling trough and prepared clay blocks | Original clay-supply layout. Processing sequence and extraction site are not reconstructed. |
| `workshop` · 8 × 6 | Low tiled room, round firing chamber, firebox, chimney, raised drying bench and vessels | Sixteenth-century ceramic-production comparison, with simplified invented kiln geometry. No claim that the shared single-input recipe recreates historical firing. |
| `storehouse` · 8 × 6 | Tall enclosed tiled store, stone damp course, high vents, braced door and sacks | Market/grain-storage function with original detached plan. Not a certified municipal granary. |
| `training` · 10 × 8 | Open earth yard, small equipment room, crossbow butts and long-pike rack | Period weapon candidates with invented dedicated training layout; targets and rack are not reconstructed artifacts. |
| `healer` · 6 × 6 | Modest tiled room, care patio, wooden treatment chair, supply table and vessel | Domestic barber/surgeon comparison, not an excavated field hospital or standard military institution. |

The timber upper patio gallery and selective market loggia separate this kit
from Roman court/peristyle imagery and Ottoman domed-civic forms. Iberian
architecture had multiple historical inheritances; this distinction does not
erase those exchanges. Later mission bell-gables, Baroque scroll fronts and a
single repeated monumental palace are not used as a universal Spanish style.

## Passive role candidates

`melee` has a plain morion-like crown/brim/comb, cuirass and long resting pike.
`ranged` has a plain cap, horizontal crossbow and bolt case. `medic` has a plain
cap, satchel and folded cloth, without a weapon or modern medical insignia. Each
has a modest doublet, breeches, hose and shoes. Costume cuts, colors and prop
proportions are artistic choices, not certified uniform or rank markings.

The [Cervantes museum's botica interpretation](https://museocasanataldecervantes.org/botica/)
places Rodrigo de Cervantes within period surgical practice and distinguishes
historical professions. Its display includes objects of different dates. Our
chair and supplies are generic abstractions; later pharmacy jars and a permanent
uniformed military medic are not claimed as verified sixteenth-century equipment.
No treatment procedure or medical effect is implemented.

Figures are original anchored, nonblocking, nonqueryable native geometry with
consistent BodyColors/body-part colors and the existing project warrior face.
The body palette is art direction, not a population-wide assertion. They have no
Humanoid, Tool, scripts, attack/healing stats or gameplay registrations. No
firearm behavior is introduced; this choice does not deny period firearms.
Roster figures do not consume workers, whose separate supplied clothing still
needs historical review.

## Verification boundary

`SpanishKit.create(kind)`, `available(kind)` and `createActor(role)` match the
existing kit API. Models are authored full size and scaled once to **0.25**;
each carries this evidence-card path. No source models or template assets are
imported or modified.

`tests/miniature-game/SpainScene.server.luau` waits up to fifteen seconds for
`MiniatureSandboxReady`, then verifies the injected fake-store boundary. It
creates temporary unparented models and checks nine catalog roles, four cardinal
rotations, every part corner within its footprint, native scale, bounded part
count, selective civic arches, timber domestic gallery, varied work props, inert
actors, body colors and period role candidates. It destroys the temporary
models and reports `SpainScenePassed/SpainSceneResult`.

Pinned StyLua and Selene pass for the kit and fixture.
